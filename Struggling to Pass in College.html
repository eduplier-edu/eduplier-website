Finding College Hard? How an Online Degree Can Help You Pass and Score Better

✍️ By Eduplier Editorial Team
📅 Updated October 2026
⏱️ 5 Min Read
🎓 Student Guide

If you keep falling behind in college, it is easy to blame yourself. But look at the routine first: strict attendance rules, a long commute every day, packed classrooms, and a final exam that decides most of your marks. Miss a couple of weeks because of a job, an illness or a family problem, and the gaps in your understanding start to pile up. That is how backlogs begin.

In many cases the problem is not ability. It is that a fixed timetable and a single high-pressure exam do not suit every learner. Online degrees from UGC-DEB entitled universities are built differently, and for many students that difference makes steady progress easier. This guide explains how, where the limits are, and how to decide if it fits you.

Where the traditional college format lets students down

  • Lectures happen once. If a topic such as a balance sheet, a coding concept or a formula is explained quickly, you cannot replay it. You are left to piece it together from friends’ notes.
  • Travel eats your energy. Two or three hours on the road each day leaves less time and focus for revision.
  • One paper carries too much weight. A bad day, poor health or exam nerves on a single date can undo months of work.
  • Attendance rules clash with real life. Working students and those with family duties often struggle to stay above the minimum.

Four ways the online model can support better results

1. You can rewatch what you didn’t understand

Online programs run on a learning platform where sessions are recorded and stored for later viewing. That changes how you study:

  • Pause and rewind tough topics until they make sense.
  • Speed through the parts you already know and slow down on the difficult ones.
  • Return to the same recording before exams instead of relying only on notes.

2. Marks are spread across the semester

Most online programs include internal or continuous assessment alongside the final exam, so not everything rides on one paper. The exact split depends on the university and the program, but a typical pattern looks like this:

Component Typical share* What it usually involves
Internal / continuous assessment Often around 30–40% Quizzes, assignments, case studies and discussions completed during the semester.
End-term exam Often around 60–70% A proctored online exam, usually taken from home on a computer.

*Indicative only. Check the exact evaluation scheme of the university you are considering.

If you submit assignments on time and put real effort into them, you can enter the final exam with a solid share of marks already earned. That does not guarantee a pass, but it takes a lot of the pressure off.

3. You choose when to study

Some people think best early in the morning, others late at night. Online learning lets you place your hardest subjects at the time you focus best, and fit study around work or family instead of the other way around.

4. Practice tools sit in one place

Many learning platforms offer unit summaries, previous-year questions and timed practice tests. Practising on the same kind of interface you will use in the real exam makes the exam day feel familiar and helps you manage your time.

💡 Counsellor tip

Treat every internal deadline as a real exam. Marks you collect during the semester give you a cushion for the final paper, and they are the easiest marks to protect.

Being honest: online study is not automatically easier

The format gives you flexibility, but not shortcuts. Before you decide, keep these points in mind:

  • You need self-discipline. Nobody takes attendance, so you have to set your own study routine.
  • The syllabus is still the syllabus. You still have to learn the subject and clear the exams.
  • You need basic equipment. A laptop or desktop with a webcam, microphone and stable internet is usually required for classes and exams.
  • Recognition matters. Choose only a university that is UGC-DEB entitled for the program you want, and confirm it on the official portal.

A simple weekly routine to score better

  1. Block fixed study hours in your calendar and treat them as non-negotiable.
  2. Watch new lectures early in the week, then revise with the recordings on the weekend.
  3. Finish assignments a day before the deadline, not on the last night.
  4. Take at least one timed practice test every week in the month before exams.
  5. Ask the university’s support team early if you get stuck on a topic.

Who is an online degree best for?

  • Working professionals who want a degree without leaving their job.
  • Students who lost a year or have backlogs and want a more flexible path forward.
  • Learners who live far from a good college or cannot commute daily.
  • Anyone who learns better at their own pace than in a fixed classroom schedule.
